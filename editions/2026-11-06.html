🔬 Ciência & Espaço

Processo digestivo do peixe-boi revela novas possibilidades para a biotecnologia

Jornal da USP

Pesquisadores do CNPEM, com participação da USP, analisaram o intestino de 47 peixes-bois, tanto amazônicos quanto marinhos, e reconstruíram mais de 6 mil genomas de bactérias. Mais da metade é de espécies que ninguém tinha descrito, e cerca de 80% dos vírus encontrados também eram novos.

No meio dessa multidão apareceu uma atividade enzimática nunca vista na natureza, a "epilactase", além de milhares de enzimas que podem ser úteis para biocombustíveis (inclusive de aviação), insumos agrícolas e saúde. O estudo saiu na Nature Microbiology.

Por que é legal: um animal calmo, que passa o dia comendo planta, carrega dentro de si uma biblioteca microbiana ainda inexplorada.

Wacky magnetic fields of ice giants may stem from conductivity changes in their deep interiors

Phys.org

When Voyager 2 flew past Uranus and Neptune in the 1980s, it found magnetic fields that are messy and steeply tilted, nothing like Earth's tidy bar magnet. A team led by UT Austin ran dynamo simulations in which the planets' interiors conduct electricity better the deeper you go, and that one assumption reproduces the strange fields.

The models also suggest the fields can wander a lot, with the tilt shifting by tens of degrees within a few decades. The researchers now want to compare this with Webb's images of the planets' auroras.

Why it's cool: the magnetic field Voyager saw may already look quite different today.

SLAC scientists find conclusive proof of a mysterious third type of magnetism

ScienceDaily

For about a century, physics recognized two main kinds of magnetism: ferromagnets like your fridge magnet, and antiferromagnets, whose internal magnetic moments cancel out. Using specialized X-ray techniques at SLAC, researchers have now firmly confirmed a third family, "altermagnets", which cancel out overall but still behave in some ways like ferromagnets.

That mix could make them useful for faster, more efficient electronics and quantum devices.

Why it's cool: it's not every day a whole new category gets added to something as old as magnetism.

🌱 Natureza & Animais

Venomous Primate Believed Extinct for 100 Years Was Hiding in Plain Sight All Along

Good News Network

Western science had no documented sighting of the Bangka slow loris since the 1930s, and in 2015 it was listed as "possibly extinct." All along, though, a local rescue center on Indonesia's Bangka Island had been quietly taking in more than 90 of them over a decade and releasing 86 back into the wild.

A new paper uses those records to confirm the species is alive, and probably more widespread on the island than anyone thought. It's also one of the very few venomous primates: it licks a toxin from glands on its arms, which mixes with its saliva and makes its bite damage cells.

Why it's cool: sometimes "lost" just means nobody asked the local people.

Alemanha testa treinamento de cães domésticos para identificar plantas invasoras

Superinteressante

Encontrar plantas invasoras cedo é caro e trabalhoso. Pesquisadores do Centro Helmholtz, na Alemanha, resolveram testar uma ajuda inesperada: cães comuns e seus tutores, sem nenhuma experiência como farejadores.

Ao longo de dois anos, 31 duplas fizeram treinamento on-line e em campo, e no fim conseguiam achar as espécies-alvo pelo cheiro com cerca de 75% de acerto. O estudo saiu na PLOS One.

Por que é legal: o passeio de domingo com o cachorro pode virar ciência cidadã.

The spring mountain butterfly has been spotted for the first time beneath power lines

Phys.org

As grazing has declined in Catalonia's mountains, forest and scrub have taken over the open meadows that the threatened butterfly Erebia epistygne needs. Researchers from the Autonomous University of Barcelona have now found it living in the cleared strips beneath high-voltage lines.

Those corridors are kept open on purpose, so they work as accidental refuges for meadow species. The team wants to use what it finds to guide how the vegetation there is managed.

Why it's cool: even a power line can end up giving a home to something fragile.

Andorinha se aconchega no ombro de artista em MG e acompanha casal que pinta quadros

Só Notícia Boa

Em Bichinho, distrito mineiro cheio de ateliês, os artistas Carla Vazarin e Ronaldo Mendes adotaram temporariamente uma andorinha filhote que provavelmente caiu do ninho. A Bibi (ou Dorinha) passa o dia empoleirada no ombro deles enquanto pintam.

O plano é cuidar dela até as asas ficarem fortes e então deixá-la voar e seguir o próprio caminho.

The Montana Ranch Where Autistic People Are Wranglers and Guides

Reasons to be Cheerful

Kathryn Nordberg kept wondering how her autistic son, Erik, would live as an adult once she was gone. In 2014 she opened Erik's Ranch, a 230-acre guest ranch near Livingston, Montana, where young autistic adults work as guides, wranglers, cooks and concierges, leading horseback rides and fly-fishing trips.

Most of them live on the property, and the goal is to swap loneliness and isolation for real jobs and community. The staff motto: "Sheets, towels and lives changed daily."

Why it's cool: one member named her chickens things like Hen Solo.

400-yo Shipwreck Puzzle Finally Cracked After More Than 20 Years of Mystery

Good News Network

A metal detectorist found a small letter padlock in the sand near Port Talbot, Wales. It came from the cargo of the Ann Francis, which sank in a storm in 1583. The lock has four rings of six letters each, which makes 1,296 possible combinations, plus a few fixed letters meant to confuse would-be thieves.

The Museum of Wales and Cardiff University put it through micro-CT scans, and on the third try they could see the internal mechanism clearly enough to read the code: ANCE.

Why it's cool: an Elizabethan PIN, read with modern X-rays.

Projeto de extensão universitária usa fliperama para ensinar ciências nas escolas

Jornal da USP

O Pinball4Science, da FEA-RP da USP, em Ribeirão Preto, vai levar alunos de cerca de 25 escolas públicas para conhecer as máquinas de fliperama por dentro, de perto. Por trás das luzes e da bolinha há eletromagnetismo, corrente contínua e alternada, relés, solenoides, transistores e lógica binária.

Alguns estudantes do ensino médio já ajudam a consertar máquinas antigas, como a fonte de uma Taito. Os encontros vão de novembro de 2026 a julho de 2027.

Por que é legal: aprender física apertando os flippers.

Muurhuizen (Wall Houses) in Amersfoort, Netherlands

Atlas Obscura

Once Amersfoort's old medieval wall stopped being needed for defense, residents started building homes right on top of it around 1500, reusing its stone. The result is a quiet, curving street of houses that follows the line of the vanished wall, mixing styles from several centuries and even taking in an old defensive tower.

Why it's cool: a whole street that's really a recycled wall.

🎨 Arte & Design

Michelangelo Used Custom Multi-Headed Brushes While Painting The Last Judgment

Smithsonian Magazine

Restorers working on the Sistine Chapel noticed brushstrokes in The Last Judgment that came in wavy, parallel groups. Their explanation is that Michelangelo used special brushes with several heads to lay down crosshatching in a single pass.

That would let him copy on the wall the hatching marks a claw chisel leaves in marble. In other words, the sculptor may have been painting the way he carved.

Why it's cool: even 500 years later, the brushstrokes still have something new to tell us.

An Animated Film Made in Excel Sums Up the Pointless Impulse to Create

Colossal

London artist Maria Tomazou made a short animated film inside a spreadsheet program, simply because she wanted to. She herself calls the project "pointless," and says the hard part was just keeping going.

Colossal's case is that this is exactly what makes it worth watching: it's a small celebration of making things for the joy of it, in the least artistic software ever.

Why it's cool: proof that you can make art in Excel too.

De Di Cavalcanti a Inezita Barroso: instituições paulistas modernizam acervos com apoio da FAPESP

Agência FAPESP

Uma chamada da FAPESP vai financiar a limpeza, a catalogação e a digitalização de acervos culturais em São Paulo. A Fundação Bienal vai tratar mais de 100 mil documentos dos dossiês de artistas, e o MAM vai criar um centro de memória com papéis históricos, como uma carta de Di Cavalcanti de 1970.

Por que é legal: um pedaço da memória cultural brasileira prestes a ficar a um clique de distância.

6 de novembro de 1892: inaugurado o Viaduto do Chá

Wikipédia

Há 134 anos, São Paulo ganhava uma ponte sobre o Vale do Anhangabaú, que naquela época ainda era coberto de plantações de chá, daí o nome. A primeira versão era de estrutura metálica e foi substituída nos anos 1930 pelo viaduto de concreto que se atravessa até hoje.

Bônus: também nasceu num 6 de novembro, em 1814, o belga Adolphe Sax, o inventor do saxofone.

Celestial nuisance

Aeon · Dylan Jow · ~3.500 palavras

Astronomers often treat cosmic dust and gas as a nuisance, the fog that has to be subtracted before you can get at the "real" physics. Dylan Jow, a radio astronomer at Stanford's Kavli Institute, argues that the fog is often the most interesting part.

He traces how astronomy came to be treated as a testing ground for fundamental physics, how big data pipelines and AI make that worse by averaging away the messy processes, and why curiosity about the particular deserves as much respect as chasing the Big Questions.
