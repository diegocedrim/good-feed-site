NASA's Swift Mission Ends, Leaves Behind Scientific Bonanza

NASA

Swift launched in 2004 on a two-year mission and kept working for more than 22 years. It made its last observation on October 20, after its orbit sank low enough that atmospheric drag kept it from pointing its telescopes.

Over that time it caught more than 2,000 gamma-ray bursts, including the first short burst with a detected afterglow. It also watched over 1,400 supernovae and saw stars being torn apart by black holes. Its work fed about 7,000 scientific papers.

It leaves 12 terabytes of public data behind, so it should keep producing discoveries for years.

NASA's asteroid Bennu samples reveal a 4.5-billion-year-old secret

ScienceDaily

A team at ETH Zurich studied half a gram of dust that OSIRIS-REx brought back from asteroid Bennu. Isotopes of iron, titanium and chromium show the asteroid formed from a mix of inner and outer Solar System material, near the line where water froze into ice.

The young Jupiter may have acted as a filter during this. It let fine dust from different regions mix and held back larger grains. Bennu's chemical fingerprint closely matches asteroid Ryugu and the rare CI meteorites.

Astronomers Detect the Oldest Fast Radio Burst, From an Ancient Galaxy

Smithsonian Magazine

South Africa's MeerKAT array caught a fast radio burst, a flash lasting a few milliseconds, and the James Webb telescope traced it to a small dwarf galaxy. The galaxy was forming stars about 3 billion years after the Big Bang.

That means the signal travelled for roughly 10 billion years, which makes it the oldest one found so far. Nobody yet knows for sure what causes these bursts, and a sample from so far back helps narrow it down.

When this light set out, neither the Sun nor the Earth existed yet.

Two Bright Green Cassowary Eggs, the First at Adelaide Zoo in 34 Years

Smithsonian Magazine

Martina and Jeffrey, a pair of southern cassowaries, produced two eggs. Breeding these big, temperamental birds in captivity is hard, and this is the zoo's first success since 1992.

Now Jeffrey takes over. Cassowary males incubate the eggs on their own for about 50 days and then raise the chicks, so the keepers are leaving him to work it out.

The neon-green shell sounds conspicuous, but on a rainforest floor it works as camouflage.

Rescuers Ensure Orphaned Baby Walrus Gets a Second Chance

Good News Network

Heavy surf drove a walrus herd off a remote beach in northern Alaska and one calf was left behind. A wildlife intern and a USGS scientist found him there and organised his rescue with help from the local community.

The calf is named Annautaq, Iñupiaq for "axe", after the intern's father's heritage. He's about five months old, weighs roughly 100 kg and gets up to six bottles a day. When his quarantine ends he'll join three older walruses who can show him how to be a walrus.

A Long-Clawed British Dinosaur, the First of Its Kind in Europe

Phys.org

A dinosaur braincase sat in London's Natural History Museum from 1924 until paleontologist Paul Barrett took a closer look at it in 2020. It turned out to belong to a new species, Invictibellatrix amberina, the "unconquered warrior of the amber".

It's the first megaraptoran found in Europe. Megaraptorans were predators with huge hand claws. This one was about 5 meters long, roughly the weight of a large tiger, and the smallest member of the group known so far.

Museum drawers still hold discoveries nobody has noticed yet.

Rebeca Andrade é tricampeã mundial no salto após pausa para cuidar da saúde

Só Notícia Boa

Depois de Paris 2024, Rebeca diminuiu o ritmo, ficou fora do Mundial de 2025 e passou um tempo cuidando do corpo e da cabeça. Ela já operou o joelho cinco vezes. Ela já tinha vencido o salto em 2021 e 2023. Agora é a primeira brasileira a ganhar a mesma prova três vezes em Mundiais, e chega a dez medalhas na competição.

Ela parou quando precisou e voltou ainda campeã.

Leitura, música e jogos aproximam idosos do conhecimento sobre o cérebro

Jornal da USP

Em Cravinhos, no interior de São Paulo, um projeto da Faculdade de Medicina de Ribeirão Preto explica neurociência para idosos em oficinas com leitura, desenho, instrumentos musicais e jogos de memória. Os encontros também servem para fazer amigos.

Mais de 50 pessoas participaram em 2026. Muitas contam que usam o que aprenderam no dia a dia e levam o assunto para a família. O projeto começou em 2016 como um curso e virou extensão porque os próprios participantes pediram mais.

Could the Voynich Manuscript's Pages Have Been Bound Out of Order All Along?

Smithsonian Magazine

Nobody has managed to read the Voynich manuscript in more than a century. Medievalist Lisa Fagin Davis and computer scientist Colin Layfield compared the vocabulary and handwriting from page to page and found that something is off. Different scribes alternate within the same gathering of pages, which medieval workshops simply didn't do.

A water stain doesn't line up across facing pages, and some drawings start on one page and continue many pages later. Their conclusion is that the bifolia were nested in the wrong order when the book was bound.

Codebreakers may have been reading the pages in the wrong order this whole time.

Frank Lloyd Wright's Only Planetarium, Restored in Florida

Smithsonian Magazine

Wright first designed this building at Florida Southern College as an observatory, then turned it into a planetarium because of Florida's humid skies. It opened in 1960. The projector died in 2004 and the dome stayed dark for about twenty years.

Almost six years of work replaced the roof, repaired water damage, added more than 280 custom tiles and refurbished the 60-seat round theater. The campus has 13 Wright buildings, the largest group of his work anywhere.

'Wildwood' Is the Longest Stop-Motion Animated Film Ever

Smithsonian Magazine

Laika, the studio behind Coraline, has made the longest stop-motion feature to date. Wildwood is set in an enchanted forest at the edge of Portland, and every frame is built by hand, down to the puppets' tiny costumes.

The piece goes behind the scenes of the handcrafted world-building and shows how much patient work sits behind every frame.

'Street Photography Africa' Turns the Street into a Stage

Colossal

Editors Nadine Barth and Azu Nwagbogu brought together 33 photographers in a book of almost 300 pages from Thames & Hudson. It covers street life in Dakar, Lagos, Abidjan, Addis Ababa and other cities.

There are musicians and dancers, everyday fashion, routines and chance encounters. The idea running through it is that the street is a stage and everyone on it is part of the performance.

Wangari Mathenge's Vibrant Paintings Re-member Kenya's 1960s

Colossal

The Nairobi-born painter fills colourful domestic interiors with figures surrounded by toys, books, maps and other symbolic objects from the years around Kenya's independence.

The series, Re-membering, draws on Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o's writing about how a culture puts itself back together. It's at Lévy Gorvy Dayan in New York and today is the last day of the show.

24 de outubro de 1932: nasce Ziraldo

Wikipédia

Ziraldo nasceu em Caratinga, Minas Gerais, e fez parte da infância de muitos brasileiros. Em 1960 lançou A Turma do Pererê, a primeira revista em quadrinhos brasileira em cores feita por um único autor. Em 1969 veio Flicts, a história de uma cor que não achava seu lugar. Em 1980, O Menino Maluquinho, o menino da panela na cabeça, que depois ganhou TV e cinema.

Ivan the Terrible Wanted to Marry Elizabeth I. How Did the Virgin Queen Say No Without Losing a Trading Partner?

Smithsonian Magazine

In the 16th century the Russian tsar wanted to marry England's queen, and England very much wanted to keep its new trade route to Moscow. This history piece draws on a new book to tell the story of that unlikely courtship.

Elizabeth turned him down so carefully that the trade survived, and she stayed unmarried for her whole 45-year reign.

A good weekend read: a 450-year-old proposal turned down without breaking the deal.
