🔬 Ciência & Espaço

NASA’s Curiosity Rover Catches Stunning Martian Dawn

NASA

Curiosity snapped a new panorama at 8:30 a.m. local Mars time, giving the most detailed view yet of a line of distant, wind-sculpted cliffs the team has been eager to reach. Low morning light brings out striking bluish tones in the foreground sand and rock.

Why it’s cool: a sunrise on another planet, photographed by a robot that has been driving for over 13 years.

Striking new images of unique supernova remnant reveal pearl-like chains

Phys.org

Pa 30, the leftover of a supernova that skywatchers recorded about 850 years ago, turns out to be laced with chains of gas knots strung like pearls — each knot wider than our whole solar system. The detail came from the Gemini North telescope, and it is the only known remnant of its kind in the Milky Way.

Why it’s cool: medieval astronomers saw the explosion; we now get to see its jewellery.

Stanford scientists found a way to regrow cartilage and stop arthritis

ScienceDaily

By blocking a protein that builds up with age, Stanford researchers got old mice to regrow lost knee cartilage and protected younger ones from arthritis after ACL-type injuries. Even more encouraging: cartilage taken from human knee-replacement patients started producing fresh, functional tissue when exposed to the same inhibitor.

Still early (mice and lab tissue), but a genuinely hopeful direction for creaky knees.

🌱 Natureza & Animais

Helping 100,000 amphibians safely cross the road

Mongabay

Every spring, salamanders and frogs in New Hampshire migrate from their woodland winter homes to the swamps where they breed — and roads stand in the way. Volunteer “Salamander Brigades” go out on rainy nights to carry them across, and over the years they have helped around 100,000 animals make the trip.

Why it’s cool: flashlights, raincoats and tiny amphibians — community science at its sweetest.

Science, Indigenous knowledge team up to restore Canada’s kelp (sea otters help, too)

Mongabay

In Clayoquot Sound, off Vancouver Island, a nursery run with the Tla-o-qui-aht First Nation has planted more than 4,200 m² of new bull kelp since early 2026 — about three and a half Olympic pools. The project mixes lab-grown kelp with traditional knowledge, and returning sea otters keep kelp-munching urchins in check.

Cientistas descobrem duas novas espécies de aranha-do-mar no Pacífico

Superinteressante

Pesquisadores canadenses acharam, no Mar de Salish, duas aranhas-do-mar inéditas — o primeiro registro do tipo na região em quase um século. Uma tem pernas cheias de espinhos, olhos vermelhos e boca triangular de três lábios, além de “ferramentas” para limpar as próprias pernas; a outra, de membros curtos, vive coberta de sujeira e parece um monstrinho do pântano.

Por que é legal: ainda há bichos novos esperando na água rasa, bem perto de grandes cidades.

Solar power helps Amazon communities ditch diesel and prepare for El Niño drought

Mongabay / AP

Off-grid solar is spreading through Brazil’s Amazon. In Nova Sociedade, near Santarém, people walked up to 20 km for water in the 2024 drought; now solar pumps supply clean water to 36 families. Over 100,000 off-grid systems were running by 2026, powering lights, schools, clinics and small businesses — and replacing noisy, costly diesel generators.

A Diver Volunteered to Clean Up Trash From a Wisconsin Lake. He Found a 1,000-Year-Old Dugout Canoe Instead

Smithsonian Magazine

A volunteer diving to pick up litter in a Wisconsin lake came across something far older than any soda can: a dugout canoe carved from white oak. Dating shows Indigenous communities near today’s Milwaukee hollowed and fire-shaped it roughly between 992 and 1049 C.E.

Why it’s cool: the best reward for picking up trash in history.

Round Barn Capital of the World in Rochester, Indiana

Atlas Obscura

Round barns enclose more space with less siding, and around 1910 they were hailed as the future of farming — builder Charles Kindig put up most of Fulton County’s 17. Then big rectangular machinery arrived and the circles fell out of fashion. Since 1989 a local museum has been rescuing and restoring the survivors.

A small, very satisfying lesson in geometry, efficiency and changing times.

“Pas-se-ar”: cães reconhecem padrões de consoantes na fala humana

Superinteressante

Um estudo da Universidade Eötvös Loránd, na Hungria, publicado na Science, mediu as ondas cerebrais de pessoas e cachorros ouvindo palavras inventadas. Os cães, como nós, parecem usar as consoantes para recortar a fala em palavras — algo antes visto só em humanos. É provável que tenham aprendido o truque convivendo com a gente.

Por que é legal: explica por que seu cachorro escuta “passear” no meio de qualquer frase.

📖 Leitura longa

How to assemble science

Aeon · Helen Pearson

A thoughtful essay on evidence synthesis — the patient work of pooling many studies instead of trusting any single one. Pearson traces it from Gene Glass’s first meta-analysis in 1976 to the Cochrane Collaboration, and shows what it costs to ignore it (the advice to put babies to sleep on their fronts lingered for decades). Her hopeful conclusion: for many hard problems, we may already know more than we think.
