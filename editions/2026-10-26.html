A 2,700-year-old solar eclipse just revealed something surprising about Earth

ScienceDaily

On July 17, 709 BCE, court astronomers in the Chinese state of Lu wrote down a total solar eclipse in the "Spring and Autumn Annals". A team led by Hisashi Hayakawa of Nagoya University found that the old capital, Qufu, actually stood about 8 km from where scholars had placed it. With that fix, the record pins down how fast Earth was spinning back then.

It confirms that days were a little shorter 2,700 years ago, because tidal friction from the Moon slowly brakes our planet. The account also hints that the Sun was just coming out of a long quiet spell and getting back to its normal 11-year cycle.

Ancient scribes ended up building a clock for modern geophysics without knowing it.

Stellar collisions may have been common when the universe was young

Phys.org

In our corner of the galaxy, stars almost never collide because there's so much empty space between them. But JWST keeps finding extremely compact star systems in the early universe, including the puzzling "little red dots". UCLA doctoral student Claire Williams modeled what happens when stars are packed that tightly.

Fast collisions tear stars apart, but slower encounters merge them, again and again, until a single giant star with the mass of a whole cluster can form. That star would then collapse into a big black hole seed, which could help explain how supermassive black holes showed up so early.

Idosos mais ativos têm volume cerebral preservado em áreas de memória e raciocínio

Agência FAPESP

Pesquisadores da Unesp, liderados por Lucas Melo Neves, reuniram 19 estudos com quase 2.700 pessoas de cerca de 70 anos e compararam 76 regiões do cérebro. Quem cumpria a recomendação da OMS, de uns 150 minutos de atividade física por semana, tinha o hipocampo de 7% a 10% maior que o dos sedentários.

O hipocampo é uma das primeiras áreas a encolher com a idade e no Alzheimer. Os estudos são observacionais, então não provam causa e efeito, mas o mecanismo faz sentido: o exercício melhora a circulação, reduz inflamação e estimula substâncias que protegem os neurônios.

Mais um bom motivo para aquela caminhada depois do café.

🌱 Natureza & Animais

Crows make decisions twice as fast as humans and monkeys

Phys.org

At the University of Tübingen, Philipp Schmidbauer and Andreas Nieder showed carrion crows a cloud of moving dots on a touchscreen and asked them to peck left or right depending on where the dots drifted. The crows answered in about 235 milliseconds on average, roughly twice as fast as people and monkeys, and were almost always right when the signal was clear.

Their brains have no layered cortex like ours, yet recordings showed the same kind of decision process building up to a threshold, just faster. The researchers think life in flight, where you have to react in a split second, pushed birds toward speed.

A completely different brain design that arrives at the same solution, only quicker.

Rare bird finding strengthens case to protect Congo rainforest across borders

Mongabay

The grey-necked picathartes is a strange, beautiful bird with a bald head painted bright blue and red. Ecologist Jean Semeki's camera traps caught one in the Luki Biosphere Reserve, the first record ever in the Democratic Republic of Congo, about 400 km south of its known range. Later sightings, including a nesting colony, turned up in the Republic of Congo and in Angola's Cabinda.

The bird lives in the Mayombe forest, which crosses four countries. Conservationists hope it becomes a flagship for a long-stalled plan to protect that forest as one cross-border park.

53 Orangutans Rescued from Fires in Borneo, With Many Rewilded to Forest Preserves

Good News Network

Since August, teams from the Indonesian nonprofit YIARI and International Animal Rescue have pulled 53 orangutans out of burning forest in Borneo, including mothers with babies and stranded youngsters. Veterinarian Karmele Llano Sanchez personally carried a young female named Irene to safety.

Many have already been released into Gunung Palung National Park. Others are still recovering at a rehab center that is now full, and the rainy season, expected in November, should help put out the fires.

Cachorrinha salva homem que caiu da escada, bateu a cabeça e desmaiou: avisou à tutora surda

Só Notícia Boa

Em Staffordshire, na Inglaterra, Gary Donally caiu da escada de madrugada e ficou desacordado. A esposa, Sandra, é surda e não ouviu nada. Quem ouviu foi Storm, uma cockapoo treinada para ajudar pessoas com deficiência auditiva: ela pulou na cama para acordar Sandra e ficou parada olhando fixo para o corredor.

Sandra entendeu o recado e encontrou o marido ao pé da escada. Gary passou 24 dias no hospital com várias fraturas no rosto, mas se recuperou. Para a família, Storm é a razão de ele estar vivo.

Good News Network

Joshua Service filmed a pile of trash dumped under a train bridge in Crown Heights, Brooklyn, and reported it to the city. When commenters told him to stop complaining and do something about it, he took them up on it. He showed up with gloves and a rake and filled about ten bags in two hours.

Hunting Red Leaves with Satellites

NASA Earth Observatory

NASA's PACE satellite was built to study ocean plankton, but its instrument splits light into 286 color bands, which is enough to tell apart the pigments in leaves: green chlorophyll, yellow-orange carotenoids and red anthocyanins. Comparing them shows, day by day, where autumn is turning the leaves red.

The maps from this season show a red wave starting in Alaska, Canada and Russia and sliding south until it reached New England and Mongolia in early October. Scientists use these maps to track drought stress and how plants respond to a changing climate, and volunteers send in ground photos to check the satellite's work.

Fall foliage watching, at the scale of a whole planet.

Flood Worries? Tape Can Block 99% of Water Compared to Sandbags

Good News Network

At the University of Hull's flood resilience lab, researchers flooded ordinary front doors up to 60 cm deep and tested different defenses. Synthetic rubber tape applied on the outside of the door frame cut leakage by 99%, and plain duct tape by 97–98%. The classic sandbag only managed about 70%.

Tape is also cheap, light, lasts on a shelf for years and goes straight in the trash afterward, while sandbags rot and get contaminated. Doors that open outward did much better too, because water pressure pushes them shut.

Sometimes the best engineering solution is already sitting in the kitchen drawer.

Wish You Were Here! Rose Sanderson's Witty Postcards Conjure a Strange Reality

Colossal

Rose Sanderson grew up in English seaside towns and collects old postcards, a habit her grandparents started. During the pandemic she began painting right on top of them: a giant octopus resting on a hotel, an enormous crab looming over the beach, a tiger lounging among the deckchairs, a sea serpent popping out of the waves.

The faded colors stay and the scale goes wild. It feels like an old monster movie with a sense of humor. The 50 paintings go on show at the Aberystwyth Arts Centre in Wales from November 11.

Great art explained: Tamara in the green Bugatti

Aeon · vídeo, 26 min

In 1929, Tamara de Lempicka painted herself at the wheel of a green Bugatti, wearing a leather helmet and gloves, with a cool, confident stare. It became one of the defining images of Art Deco and of the modern woman of the Jazz Age.

In this episode of Great Art Explained, James Payne follows her from a wealthy childhood in Poland to her escape from the Russian Revolution and on to 1920s Paris. He shows how she mixed sharp geometry with glamour to paint women who drive themselves wherever they want to go.

26 de outubro de 1942: nasce Milton Nascimento

Wikipédia

Milton nasceu no Rio, mas foi adotado e criado em Três Pontas, no sul de Minas, por Lília, uma professora de música. Ganhou o apelido de Bituca por causa do biquinho que fazia quando criança. Em 1967, "Travessia", feita com Fernando Brant, ficou em segundo lugar num festival e apresentou aquela voz ao Brasil.

Depois vieram o Clube da Esquina com Lô e Márcio Borges em Belo Horizonte, "Maria Maria", "Canção da América", "Coração de Estudante" e muitas outras. Hoje ele faz 84 anos.

Bhutan: the last place

Aeon · documentário de Alexis Bloom e Tshewang Dendup · 22 min

Until 1999, Bhutan was the only country in the world without television. This short 2002 documentary, made for PBS Frontline, visits the Himalayan Buddhist kingdom right after the ban was lifted and talks with kids, shopkeepers and monks about what changed.

Suddenly there are boys imitating American pro wrestlers and new cravings for brand-name clothes. As one official puts it, people started wanting things they never knew existed. The film is less a lament than a gentle portrait of a culture meeting the outside world all at once.

A time capsule that makes you look at your own screen a little differently.
