🔬 Ciência & Espaço

A lone, starless cloud may be a "dark" galaxy

Phys.org

Using China's giant FAST radio telescope, a team led by Jin-Long Xu found a cloud of neutral hydrogen, J1351+0039, holding about half a million suns' worth of gas and no visible stars at all. Judging by how the gas moves, the whole thing weighs around 130 million solar masses, so most of it is probably dark matter.

It may be a "failed galaxy": a clump that never gathered enough cold gas to light up. Earlier candidates sat close to big galaxies, which muddied the picture. This one floats alone, which makes it the cleanest example yet. Deep images from Hubble or Webb should settle whether it is truly starless.

A galaxy-sized object that may have spent its whole life in the dark.

Acordo amplia presença paulista no maior levantamento astronômico da história

Agência FAPESP

O Observatório Vera C. Rubin, no Chile, vai passar dez anos fotografando o céu do Sul sem parar. O projeto LSST deve catalogar cerca de 20 bilhões de galáxias e 17 bilhões de estrelas. Um novo acordo entre a FAPESP e o consórcio americano Aura dobra a participação de São Paulo: agora são 10 pesquisadores principais, cada um com até 5 jovens cientistas, somando 60 pessoas com acesso total aos dados.

Parte da mágica também é brasileira. A rede acadêmica Rednesp leva os dados do Chile aos Estados Unidos em cerca de 7 segundos, a 400 Gbps, o que permite emitir alertas quase em tempo real quando algo muda no céu.

NASA's Roman Space Telescope is sharper than planned by nearly 30%

ScienceDaily

Roman launched at the end of August, and engineers have just finished fine-tuning its optics. The goal for its main camera was a wavefront error of at most 90 nanometers. After nudging the secondary mirror by roughly a sixteenth of the width of a human hair, they measured 63.

Sharper images mean better wide surveys of the sky, which is exactly what Roman was built for: mapping huge regions to study dark matter and dark energy.

The difference between "good" and "much better" was a movement smaller than a hair.

Desmatamento na Amazônia cai 41,8% e chega ao menor nível em 38 anos; Cerrado também

Só Notícia Boa

Os números do Inpe para agosto de 2025 a julho de 2026 mostram que a área desmatada na Amazônia caiu de 5.731 km² para 3.335 km². É o menor valor desde que o monitoramento começou, em 1988. No Cerrado, a queda foi de 23,8%, para cerca de 5.517 km², o menor índice desde 2001.

Mais fiscalização e a criação de novas unidades de conservação aparecem entre os fatores apontados para a queda.

São quase 2.400 km² de floresta a mais de pé em um ano, uma área maior que a da cidade de São Paulo.

North America's largest wildlife overpass is working: collisions on I-25 down 91%

Good News Network

Near Larkspur, Colorado, a planted bridge about 200 feet wide carries animals over a busy interstate, with five underpasses nearby. That stretch of road used to average about one wildlife collision a day. In the first ten months after opening, crashes dropped 91%.

Cameras have caught bears, elk, moose, rabbits and even leopard frogs using it. A pronghorn crossed there for the first time in some 30 years. Colorado is now planning six more overpasses.

How a snail's shell decides to coil left or right

Phys.org

Most snails spiral to the right, but in one Japanese mountain valley, left- and right-coiling Euhadra snails live side by side. Comparing them with pond snails, researchers traced the difference to natural variation in a myosin gene. Myosin is a protein that helps cells move and change shape.

Tiny changes in it tip the very first cell divisions to twist one way or the other, and the whole animal follows. Both versions come out perfectly healthy.

Left- and right-handedness in a body can start with one protein in the first few cells.

Museu Nacional ganha mais R$ 10 milhões para a reconstrução e já recebe visitantes

Só Notícia Boa

Oito anos depois do incêndio de 2018, o Paço de São Cristóvão, no Rio, segue renascendo. Desde junho, o palácio já está aberto ao público com duas exposições gratuitas, "Bastidores da Ciência" e "Rescaldo das Memórias". Agora a obra ganhou mais R$ 10 milhões, que se somam a recursos públicos e privados do projeto Museu Nacional Vive.

As obras trouxeram até surpresas: os alicerces de uma antiga capela da família real portuguesa. Há também obras de Vik Muniz feitas com cinzas e fragmentos resgatados do incêndio.

Um museu de história natural que virou, ele mesmo, uma história de recomeço.

The uplifting story of one of Zimbabwe's pioneering women rangers

Mongabay

Nyaradzo Auxilia Hoto grew up poor in the Zambezi Valley. In 2017, after leaving a difficult marriage, she joined Akashinga, an all-women ranger force. She made sergeant by 2019, studied while she worked, and in 2023 graduated in wildlife ecology and conservation.

Today, at 34, she is a biodiversity officer who turns the rangers' field data into conservation decisions, and she won an IUCN International Ranger Award along the way. Her motto for other women: no situation is permanent.

Indonesian women cash in on mangrove restoration

Mongabay

In Sarawet, a coastal village in North Sulawesi, a women's group called Rhizopora has been restoring mangroves since 2022. This year alone they planted 30,000 trees in eight months. The forest pays them back. They sell seedlings, harvest mud crabs that thrive among the roots, and run a small savings-and-loans scheme.

Leader Wisye Sahambangung says the village is now known well beyond its shores. One member has used her earnings to buy a house and start a business.

Protecting the coast and earning a living turned out to be the same job.

Rabbits were a medieval royal luxury in England, until the Black Death made them commonplace

Phys.org

Rabbits only really arrived in England with the Normans. For a couple of centuries they were a status symbol, costing four or five times as much as a chicken. Lords kept them in guarded warrens, complete with lodges and watchtowers, and gave them as royal gifts.

After the Black Death, survivors had more land and better wages. Ordinary people started eating rabbit and wearing its fur. Medieval scribes found the whole thing funny, too: manuscript margins from the period show rabbits turning the tables on hunters.

An extinct crocodilian's skull was found in the rocks that built the Sphinx, and it's also missing its nose

Good News Network

A 45-million-year-old skull from Egypt's Mokattam Formation sat in a museum for over a century, filed under the wrong name. An international team led by Paul Burke, of the Swedish Museum of Natural History, re-examined it and found a new kind of gavial, the narrow-snouted relative of crocodiles.

The tip of its snout is gone, and it came from the same limestone the ancient Egyptians quarried to build the Great Sphinx. So the researchers named it Herugavialis cairensis, after the god Heru, in a nod to the Sphinx's famous missing nose.

Paleontologists with a sense of humor.

Madrid's renewed Sorolla Museum lets the "master of light" back into view

Smithsonian Magazine

Joaquín Sorolla painted Mediterranean sunlight on beaches and wet skin like almost nobody else. His Madrid museum is in the house and studio where he lived and worked. It has just finished the first phase of a multi-year renovation.

The expanded galleries mean more of his canvases can come out of storage and be seen again, inside the rooms where many of them were made.

If you ever go to Madrid, this one is a quiet, sunny escape from the big museums.

Rembrandt turned ancient myths into "supremely human" works of art

Smithsonian Magazine

Coming soon to the Metropolitan Museum of Art, "Rembrandt and the Ancients" looks at a less-explored side of the Dutch master: how he drew on Greek and Roman stories. The show hangs his paintings and drawings next to the classical sources that inspired them.

The thread running through it is how Rembrandt took gods and heroes and gave them ordinary faces, doubts and weight. One example is his Aristotle, resting a thoughtful hand on a bust of Homer.

17 de outubro de 1849: o último dia de Chopin

Wikipédia

Há 177 anos, Frédéric Chopin morria em Paris, aos 39 anos. Deixou noturnos, mazurcas, valsas e baladas que mudaram para sempre o que um piano podia dizer. Ele viveu longe da Polônia natal a maior parte da vida adulta e pediu que seu coração voltasse para casa. A irmã o levou escondido para Varsóvia, e ele está até hoje num pilar da Igreja da Santa Cruz.

Dam removals are bringing fish back across the northeastern United States

Smithsonian Magazine

The big dam removals in the American West get the headlines. Meanwhile, New England is quietly taking down hundreds of small, forgotten dams, many of them left over from old mills, that have blocked streams for generations.

This Smithsonian feature follows what happens when the barriers come down: rivers find their old shape again, and river herring come back in droves to spawn where their ancestors did. A slow, hopeful read for a Saturday morning.
