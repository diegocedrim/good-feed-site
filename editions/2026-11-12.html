Pesquisa brasileira transforma chorume em fertilizante seguro para a agricultura

Superinteressante · Agência FAPESP

O Brasil importa quase todo o potássio que usa nas lavouras. Pesquisadores da Unicamp descobriram como precipitar quimicamente, a partir do chorume de aterros sanitários, a carnalita — um mineral rico em potássio e magnésio que normalmente só se obtém com mineração profunda. Junto com a estruvita, já conhecida, o processo entrega nitrogênio, fósforo, potássio e magnésio às plantas, e os testes foram feitos com alface.

O líquido mais nojento do lixão virando comida para a horta.

Earth's fiery birth may have created hundreds of oceans' worth of water

ScienceDaily · University of New Mexico

A team at the University of New Mexico heated silicate rock to 1,200–1,400 °C in flowing hydrogen to mimic pebbles falling through the young Earth's atmosphere. In minutes, most of the iron oxide turned into metallic iron and released water vapor — something that didn't happen at all in helium. Scaled up, the reaction could have made hundreds of oceans' worth of water.

Maybe our water wasn't delivered by comets after all — maybe planets simply brew it.

Ant colonies defy aging patterns as death risk falls and reproduction rises

Phys.org · University of Houston

Biologists Blaine Cole and Diane Wiernasz followed 3,285 western harvester ant colonies in Colorado for 34 years. The older a colony got, the less likely it was to die and the more likely it was to reproduce — so-called negative senescence. The trick: workers live about a year and are constantly replaced, so the colony's "body" stays young while the colony itself ages.

A superorganism that gets better with age, like a good cast-iron pan.

🌱 Natureza & Animais

Genetic study describes two new species of Amazonian howler monkeys

Mongabay

Combining genomes, body traits and old museum specimens, a team led by Romina Batista (Salford and Brazil's INPA) split the red howler into new species: the Pantepui red howler (Alouatta iroi) in the northwestern Amazon and the Mato Grosso red howler (A. matogrossensis) in the southern Brazilian Amazon. Trinidad's howlers may be a third. Naming them is the first step to protecting them — the Mato Grosso one may already qualify as critically endangered.

Our bugios-ruivos just turned out to be more than one kind.

Brown Bear Population in Spain Doubles With Incredible Increase of its Breeding Range

Good News Network

Cantabrian brown bears, once down to as few as 140 animals, now number about 370 and roam some 17,000 km² of northern Spain. Protections, new reserves and quieter countryside let their breeding range keep expanding — by 77% in just the last decade studied. Researchers say management plans are now underestimating how far the bears can go.

A comeback that's outpacing the people planning for it.

Expedição científica revela a biodiversidade no topo da Amazônia

Revista Pesquisa FAPESP

Dezesseis pesquisadores passaram 19 dias acampados a até 1.260 metros de altitude na serra do Aracá, um tepui no norte do Amazonas. Voltaram com cinco possíveis lagartos novos, uma cobra inédita, cerca de dez prováveis anfíbios desconhecidos e entre 7 mil e 11 mil insetos. As amostras ainda vão render anos de estudos — de genética a micróbios do solo.

Ainda existem "mundos perdidos" no Brasil, e tem gente subindo lá para conhecer.

Uma medalhinha de olimpíada de matemática que abriu um caminho inteiro.

Jovens que foram amigos por correspondência se reencontram após 24 anos e se casam

Só Notícia Boa

David e Jodie se conheceram nas férias na ilha grega de Kos, em 1994, e viraram amigos por carta até perderem contato. Em 2016, uma mensagem de aniversário dela chegou ao celular da mãe dele, e o fio foi reatado. Cartas de papel têm um prazo de validade bem mais longo do que parece.

100-Year-Old 'Store Mom' Surprised With Her First Birthday Party

Good News Network

After a stroke, Gretchen Sollo started walking laps inside a Lowe's hardware store in Bradley, Illinois — about 1,500 miles over five years. The staff adopted her as their "store mom," and when she turned 100 they lined an aisle with a blue carpet, balloons, cake and a personalized vest. It was the first birthday party of her life.

Community can grow in the most unlikely aisle.

Em 1886, alunos de Direito recepcionaram Sarah Bernhardt em São Paulo

Jornal da USP

Quando a maior atriz do mundo veio a uma São Paulo de uns 60 mil habitantes para encenar A Dama das Camélias e Fedra, quem a ciceroneou foram estudantes abolicionistas do Largo São Francisco. Esse encontro curioso virou a peça Sarah Bernhardt em São Paulo, de Samir Yazbek, com direção de Ulysses Cruz, em cartaz no Sesc Santana até 22 de novembro.

Um pedacinho esquecido da história paulistana — e um programa para o fim de semana.

Coventry Cathedral's Stained Glass Is 'a Medieval Jigsaw Puzzle' — Its 12,000 Fragments Have Been Digitized

Smithsonian Magazine

Coventry's 15th-century windows have had a rough life: broken during the Reformation, then taken down and stored for safekeeping in the 1940s. What survives is a puzzle of roughly 12,000 pieces, and now every fragment is online for anyone to browse. Among the saints and Bible scenes are funny little animals and portraits of the medieval donors who paid for the glass.

A 600-year-old jigsaw you can now explore from the sofa.

Scientists uncover a 192-year-old mystery hidden in a mangled silver coin

ScienceDaily

A badly crushed 1834 sixpence turned up in an album bought whole at a car boot sale near Manchester. Microscopy, chemical tests and 3D surface mapping showed the dent in the king's portrait came from repeated, concentrated pressure — most likely a mistake at the mint itself, not wear or a later knock. Labs at Brunel and Oxford helped confirm the measurements.

Forensic science applied to a single flea-market coin.

Colossal

This year's top prize went to Ole Bielfeldt of Cologne, who built his own rig to photograph tiny droplets flying inside an inkjet printer. Other winners include a jumping spider dusted with pollen, glowing mouse neurons, a tardigrade, a snowflake and a daisy lit only by its own natural fluorescence.

Imagining the Mossy Cities of the Future

Reasons to be Cheerful

German artist Philip Wallisfurth has been making "bio-graffiti" out of living moss since 2012, softening grey public spaces from Aachen to Berlin. Moss drinks through its surface, filters air and dampens noise — and cities are experimenting: moss roofs in Seoul cut peak surface temperatures by about 19 °C, and Berlin has a 1,200 m² vertical moss farm. The catch: it only cools while it stays damp.

Graffiti that breathes.

The Invisible Lamplighter in The Hague, Netherlands

Atlas Obscura

Tucked into the Mallemolen, a quiet alley in The Hague, a bronze sculpture shows a lamplighter mid-leap up a lamppost — turning the humble gas-lamp chore into acrobatics. Little bronze birds perch on the lantern, keeping him company.

A forgotten job, remembered with a sense of humor.

📜 Neste dia

12–13 de novembro de 1833 — a chuva de meteoros Leônidas explodiu numa verdadeira tempestade sobre a América do Norte, com estimativas de mais de cem mil meteoros por hora. O espetáculo foi registrado por vários povos nativos e levou o astrônomo Denison Olmsted a propor que os meteoros vinham de uma nuvem de partículas no espaço, e não da atmosfera — o começo da ciência moderna dos meteoros.

Why anything exists

Aeon · Andrew T Brenner · ~22 min

Why is there something rather than nothing? Philosopher Andrew Brenner pokes at the old assumption that "nothing" would be the simplest option: even an empty world would still contain truths of logic, mathematics and morality. If so, nothingness isn't so simple after all — and a world with something in it might be the simplest possible one. He admits it doesn't settle why reality looks the way it does, but it's a lovely puzzle to sit with.

The biggest question there is, in coffee-sized steps.
