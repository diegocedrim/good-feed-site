All 5 genetic "letters" of DNA and RNA discovered in a 4.5-billion-year-old asteroid

ScienceDaily

Japanese researchers analysing grains of asteroid Ryugu — brought home by the Hayabusa2 probe in 2020 — found all five nucleobases used by life: adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine and uracil. In 2023 the same team had spotted only uracil; now, with ultra-clean extraction, all five turned up in both samples, in similar amounts.

Together with NASA's Bennu samples, which showed the same set last year, it suggests the raw alphabet of genetics was scattered all over the early Solar System, possibly raining down on the young Earth.

Why it's cool: from just 5.4 grams of space dust, a hint that life's ingredients are cosmic commonplace.

A hidden star is plowing through Betelgeuse's atmosphere, new evidence suggests

ScienceDaily

Betelgeuse, the red giant on Orion's shoulder, seems to have a small companion — nicknamed Siwarha — orbiting inside its puffy outer atmosphere, at about twice the star's radius. Five years of radio observations with the VLA and ALMA show the atmosphere stretching and changing shape in step with where the companion should be.

The partner is far too faint to see directly, but its wake may help explain Betelgeuse's roughly six-year brightness cycle and how old giant stars shed material.

Why it's cool: a star swimming through another star's skin.

🌱 Natureza & Animais

A 50-year-old mystery of vanishing frogs reveals a hidden killer

ScienceDaily

Between 1979 and 1982, frogs at the Boracéia Biological Station, in São Paulo's Atlantic Forest, simply disappeared. Testing more than 370 museum specimens, Smithsonian and Penn State researchers found no chytrid fungus before 1979 — and infection in over half of the frogs collected in the 1980s.

The hopeful part: on recent visits (2022–2024) they found 12 of the 17 species again. The fungus is still there, so the frogs seem to have evolved tolerance over generations.

Why it's cool: old jars in a museum solved a Brazilian mystery — and the ending is a comeback.

Photograph helps identify a West African bird not seen in 13 years

Mongabay

In Côte d'Ivoire's Taï National Park, researcher Stephanie Bartlett snapped a photo of a shy little bird before she had even seen it properly — and the picture turned out to be the Ghana cuckooshrike, orange-bodied with black-and-yellow wings. It's the first confirmed record since 2013.

Nobody knew whether the species was extinct, rare or just very good at hiding; now there's a lead, and another reason to protect Taï's rainforest.

Loch Ness Is About to Welcome a New Monster Not Seen There in 500 Years — the Beaver!

Good News Network

Scotland's NatureScot has licensed two charities to release up to 15 beaver families around the River Ness and its famous loch over three years. The last record of beavers there dates from 1526–27.

Beavers build wetlands, boost biodiversity and soften both floods and droughts — and more than 73% of locals consulted backed the plan.

Why it's cool: Loch Ness finally gets a monster that actually shows up.

Wild animals can now GPS-collar themselves for research

Phys.org

Norwegian researchers built baited stations that slip an adjustable GPS collar onto red deer and red foxes as they feed — no chasing, darting or trapping. The collars drop off on their own after weeks or months.

Less stress for the animals, lower costs for scientists, and potentially more honest data on how wildlife really behaves.

Professora de 96 anos lança primeiro livro com mais de 100 poemas escritos à mão, no RS

Só Notícia Boa

Ema Vargas, professora aposentada, passou a vida escrevendo poemas num caderno de memórias — e agora, aos 96 anos, eles viraram livro: Eu Venho Oferecer Meu Coração, lançado na Feira do Livro de Porto Alegre.

São mais de cem textos sobre o tempo, o afeto e o que ficou marcado. Nas horas vagas, ela dança tango e não aceita que a idade diga o que alguém pode ou não fazer.

Por que é legal: nunca é tarde para estrear.

Woman Becomes First Person to Swim the Entire California Coastline

Good News Network

Catherine Breed set off from the Oregon border on July 1 and touched the Mexican border on October 19: about 900 miles of open ocean in 111 days. Along the way came tropical storms, seven-foot waves, contrary currents and eight shark encounters — but also sea lions, dolphins and whales for company.

She stopped regularly to give talks on ocean conservation, and hopes the swim nudges women and girls toward their own impossible goals.

🛠️ Tecnologia, História & Curiosidades

CT scans reveal four-letter code to 400-year-old lock from Welsh shipwreck

Phys.org

The Ann Francis sank in a storm off the Welsh coast in December 1583, carrying a small brass combination lock that probably guarded a chest of valuables. Too fragile to fiddle with, the lock went into a micro-CT scanner at Cardiff University.

The 3D images showed exactly how the internal notches lined up — and the Elizabethan password turned out to be ANCE. It's on display in Swansea until September 2027.

Why it's cool: cracking a 443-year-old PIN without touching it.

Como os romanos usaram nanotecnologia para fazer um cálice de vidro mudar de cor

Superinteressante

A Taça de Licurgo, peça romana guardada no Museu Britânico, é verde-jade quando a luz bate de frente e vira vermelho-rubi quando iluminada por trás. O segredo está no próprio vidro, que traz partículas minúsculas de metal espalhando a luz de um jeito diferente conforme o ângulo.

A taça ainda tem uma “gaiola” de vidro vazado com a cena mítico-dionisíaca de Licurgo preso por uma videira — e talvez a troca de cor seja uma piscadela ao amadurecer das uvas.

Por que é legal: nanotecnologia, uns 1.600 anos antes de existir a palavra.

Collins Obijiaku's Tender Portraits Conjure Memories and Community

Colossal

Nigerian-born, Chicago-based painter Collins Obijiaku mixes crisp line work with loose brushstrokes and flat, calm backgrounds; his sitters look straight back at you. Smells and sounds that pull him toward home shape the scenes, from echoes of Renaissance altarpieces to an aunt and a small boy against a lavender wall.

His show Wherever You Are, Remember Me opens November 7 in Los Angeles.

In an Updated Edition, Photographer Ivan McClellan Returns to Black Rodeo Culture

Colossal

Since 2015, Ivan McClellan has followed Black rodeo communities across the US, earning the trust that shows in every frame. The new edition of Eight Seconds adds 91 images, trading harsh arena light for warm shadows and quiet, respectful portraits.

The cover belongs to Kortnee Solomon, a Texan teen and one of the youngest riders, standing tall beside her horses.

4 de novembro de 1869: nasce a revista Nature

Wikipédia

Há 157 anos, o astrônomo Norman Lockyer e o editor Alexander Macmillan publicavam o primeiro número da Nature, hoje uma das revistas científicas mais importantes do mundo. Foi nela que saiu, em 1953, o artigo de Watson e Crick sobre a dupla hélice do DNA — que, curiosamente, foi publicado sem revisão por pares.

The Four-Color Theorem Has Haunted Mathematicians for Decades. It Just Got a Rare New Proof, but Some Still Long for a Simpler Solution

Smithsonian Magazine

Can every map be painted with just four colours so that no two neighbouring regions match? The question sounds like a children's puzzle, yet it took more than a century and a computer-assisted proof to settle — one that many mathematicians never found truly satisfying.

This feature follows the story up to a rare new proof, and asks why so many still dream of a short, elegant argument a human could hold in their head. Perfect for a slow second coffee.
