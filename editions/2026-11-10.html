Scientists finally discover why we learn to love bitter coffee

ScienceDaily

Nobody likes their first coffee, so why do so many of us end up loving it? A UC San Francisco team, publishing in Nature, found that in mice a slow dopamine signal builds in a brain region called the amygdala several minutes after the gut detects nutrients — and that signal teaches the brain to link a flavor with calories.

Blocking the signal stopped the learning; switching it on artificially made mice prefer a flavor with no calories at all. Brain scans of human volunteers point to the same region doing similar work.

Why it's cool: your gut has been quietly training your taste buds — read this with your cup in hand.

Voyager 1 is about to reach one light-day from Earth

Smithsonian Magazine

Launched in 1977, Voyager 1 is the farthest object humans have ever made, and it's about to cross a poetic milestone: so far away that its radio signals, travelling at the speed of light, take a full day to reach us.

Nearly 50 years into the journey, the spacecraft and its twin are still sending data home from beyond the edge of the Sun's bubble, on a power supply that engineers keep stretching with clever tricks.

Why it's cool: a message sent "good morning" arrives the next morning.

Por que o espelho do banheiro desembaça de baixo para cima?

Superinteressante

A resposta é contraintuitiva: o ar úmido é mais leve que o ar seco. Uma molécula de água pesa menos (18 g/mol) que a média do ar (cerca de 29 g/mol), então o vapor quente sobe e se acumula no alto do banheiro.

Quando o chuveiro desliga, o ar seco e mais denso entra por baixo, e as gotinhas da parte inferior do espelho evaporam primeiro.

Por que é legal: física que você vai observar amanhã de manhã.

World's Driest Desert Erupts into Record-Breaking Bloom

Good News Network

Chile's Atacama, the driest non-polar place on Earth, is covered in flowers: about 200 species are blooming, some in spots that hadn't flowered since before 2000. Record rains soaked deep into the soil and woke up seeds that had waited decades.

Officials call it the widest, most vivid bloom in 30 years, and it's pulling in pollinators, reptiles, bats and guanacos. A local seed bank is collecting seeds while the show lasts — just two or three months.

Why it's cool: patience, at a geological scale.

Tens of thousands of orchids saved from road development in southern India

Mongabay

When trees in Kerala were marked for felling to widen a road, researchers didn't just let the orchids living on their branches go with them. They removed more than 27,000 of these epiphytic plants and replanted them elsewhere.

Two years later, about three-quarters of them are still alive — a striking success rate for such delicate plants.

Fish Stocks Thrive Thanks to This Pink Seaweed and Locals Who Protected It

Good News Network

Maerl is a slow-growing pink seaweed that builds crunchy, coral-like beds on the seafloor, perfect nurseries for young fish. In parts of Scotland, local people pushed to ban scallop dredging, which had been tearing those beds apart.

Ten years later, the protected seabeds are recovering and fish are coming back with them.

Why it's cool: leave the sea alone a little and it rebuilds itself.

Livro resgata trajetória de motorista que ajudou a revolucionar a arqueologia brasileira

Jornal da USP

Por mais de cinco décadas, João Bárbara Filho, o Seu João, foi motorista da UFMG, e esteve nos bastidores de algumas das maiores descobertas da arqueologia brasileira, como a missão que revelou Luzia, o fóssil humano mais antigo das Américas, em Lagoa Santa.

Chamado pelos arqueólogos de "engenheiro de campo", ele inventava equipamentos e transformou sua casa em Mocambeiro em base para pesquisadores. Aos 92 anos, segue ativo e com memória afiada, e agora ganhou um livro: Nosso João, de Rodrigo Elias Oliveira (MAE-USP).

Por que é legal: a ciência também é feita por quem dirige o carro até o sítio.

Só Notícia Boa

No Einstein, em São Paulo, pelo SUS, o cirurgião Fábio Gaiotto implantou um coração de doador em Marcelo, de 20 anos, sem retirar o coração doente. Os dois órgãos funcionaram juntos por 35 dias, até a pressão pulmonar do rapaz baixar.

Só então a equipe retirou o coração original numa segunda cirurgia. Depois de cerca de quatro meses internado, Marcelo voltou para casa, a andar e a tocar piano.

Por que os gatos gostam de entrar em caixas?

Superinteressante

Todo mundo que tem gato já viu: o presente fica de lado e a caixa vira o brinquedo. A explicação junta várias coisas — a memória de filhote num ninho fechado e seguro, o instinto de se esconder de predadores enquanto vigia presas.

E tem até medição: pesquisadores usam uma "pontuação de estresse felino", e gatos com acesso a caixas tendem a ficar mais calmos.

The wholehearted life

Aeon · Ben Daitz · ~5,400 words

A portrait of Roshi Joan Halifax — Zen teacher, medical anthropologist and long-time companion to the dying — by a writer who has known her for years. It follows her from a sickly childhood through fieldwork, a turbulent spell in psychedelic research and a personal collapse, to Buddhist practice and caring for people at the end of life.

Along the way it asks how to be compassionate without burning out, an idea she calls "pathological altruism". Slow, thoughtful, a good one for the weekend.
