Meet Earth's New 'Mini-Moon' 2026 QQ8

Smithsonian Magazine

Earth has picked up a temporary companion: a rock roughly six feet across, named 2026 QQ8, that our planet's gravity captured back in April. It was spotted in August, and astronomers spent weeks making sure it was a natural asteroid and not a stray piece of space junk. It should drift off again around December or January.

Por que é legal: a Terra tem, por alguns meses, uma segunda lua do tamanho de um sofá.

Physicists just sent an unblockable message through a brick wall using gravity

ScienceDaily

Researchers spelled out the word "Gravity" by shuttling an old elevator's counterweight between two positions. On the other side of a brick wall, an extremely sensitive detector picked up the tiny shifts in gravitational pull and decoded the message. It's slow, but nothing can block it.

Por que é legal: um "telégrafo gravitacional" feito com peça de elevador velho.

Where Does Language Originate in the Brain?

Smithsonian Magazine

For about 150 years, textbooks have taught that Wernicke's area, a patch of brain just above the left ear, is mainly about understanding language. A new study suggests it also plays a real role in producing speech. If it holds up, a classic diagram of the brain may need redrawing.

A prickly surprise: Mexican hairy dwarf porcupines glow under UV light

Phys.org

Shine ultraviolet light on a Mexican hairy dwarf porcupine and it glows. Researchers have now properly documented this photoluminescence in the species. Until now, most records of glowing mammals came from museum specimens, and reports from living porcupines were only anecdotes.

Por que é legal: tem bicho por aí com um "modo neon" que a gente nem enxerga.

Species down to the last 50 animals can recover, but rarely without help

Mongabay

A study looked at 141 birds and mammals that fell below 50 wild individuals. Of those that got direct conservation help, 61% climbed back to at least 100 animals; of those left alone, only two recovered. The Mauritius kestrel, once almost gone, is one of the comeback stories.

Por que é legal: prova em números de que ajudar funciona — mesmo no limite.

Clouded tiger cats are notoriously hard to catch. This young researcher caught seven

Mongabay

Since 2023, PhD student Amaia Autor has captured and fitted GPS collars to seven clouded tiger cats, small and elusive wild cats of Costa Rica's Talamanca Mountains. She is the first person known to capture free-ranging animals of this species for research, which opens a window into lives nobody had tracked before.

Só Notícia Boa

Uma técnica da Universidade Federal do Ceará usa pele de tilápia tratada para reparar lesões na córnea. O método já passou de mil animais atendidos — incluindo aves e bichos silvestres — e salvou olhos que, de outra forma, teriam de ser removidos.

Viral Infections Fall 68% in Brazil After Releasing Wolbachia-Infected Mosquitoes

Good News Network

A four-year trial in Brazil released mosquitoes carrying Wolbachia, a bacterium that keeps them from passing on dengue, Zika and chikungunya. Schoolchildren in the areas studied had a 68% lower risk of catching these viruses. Fighting mosquitoes with better mosquitoes, it turns out, works.

Retired Teacher Turns Retired Musical Instruments into Bird Feeders

Good News Network

On Prince Edward Island, in Canada, retired teacher Rob Burke gives old, broken instruments a second life as bird feeders. Guitars and other castoffs now hold seed for the neighbourhood birds, and he calls the whole setup the "Frets and Feathers Cafe".

Por que é legal: aposentados ajudando instrumentos aposentados a alimentar passarinhos.

Genetic sovereignty in archaeology inspired the creation of the first ancient DNA laboratory in Brazil

Jornal da USP

USP's Museum of Archaeology and Ethnology has opened Brazil's first laboratory dedicated to ancient DNA. The idea is to study Latin America's past here, building a regional research network, instead of shipping samples abroad. Its first projects look at the Luso-African roots of an island community in Amapá.

Indigenous Hunters Drove Bison Off This Cliff for 3,000 Years

Smithsonian Magazine

Two science teachers out on a hike in Caprock Canyons State Park stumbled on hundreds of ancient bison bones. The site turned out to be a "buffalo jump", used by Indigenous hunters for some 3,000 years, and only the second ever found in Texas. Fittingly, the park is home to the Texas State Bison Herd today.

Wasserspeier ('Water Spitter') in Bern, Switzerland

Atlas Obscura

Since 1984, a dragon-like spout perched on a rooftop in Bern's old town has been squirting water at passers-by — especially the ones who bend down to grab the five-franc coins scattered on the street below. The artist and the homeowner put it back up every spring.

Por que é legal: uma pegadinha urbana que já dura mais de 40 anos.
