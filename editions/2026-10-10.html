Discovery marks the first detection of variable water clouds outside of the solar system

Phys.org

WISE 0855 is a brown dwarf, a Jupiter-sized "failed star" just 7.5 light-years away, and at about −8 °C it is colder than a winter morning on Earth. A University of Arizona team led by Brittany Miles watched it with the James Webb Space Telescope for 11 hours, taking a spectrum every 15 minutes as it rotated.

The water clouds high in its atmosphere visibly thickened and thinned as different patches turned into view, while gases like carbon monoxide and phosphine were churned up from deep below. It is the first time anyone has seen water clouds change over time on a world outside the Solar System.

Weather, in the literal sense, on a world nobody has ever visited.

A quantum prediction from 1931 just came to life

ScienceDaily

In 1931, physicist Hans Bethe worked out on paper that particles squeezed into a single line could clump into strange bound groups, now called "Bethe strings", held together without any chemical bond. Nearly a century later, a team at the University of Innsbruck finally made them.

They cooled cesium atoms to almost absolute zero and trapped them in thousands of tiny one-dimensional tubes, where clusters of six or more atoms formed and could even bump into each other without falling apart. When the atoms were let loose in 3D, the strings broke up, and the energy released was the fingerprint that proved they had been there.

A 95-year-old pencil-and-paper idea, now sitting on a lab bench.

Pacientes que ficaram cegos voltam a detectar objetos com terapia genética e óculos especiais

Só Notícia Boa

Pesquisadores da Universidade de Pittsburgh e de um instituto de oftalmologia em Basileia testaram um tratamento para a retinose pigmentar, doença hereditária que destrói as células da retina que captam luz. Uma injeção no olho ensina outras células da retina a produzir uma proteína sensível à luz, e óculos com câmera traduzem o ambiente em pulsos de luz que essas células conseguem “ler”.

Dos dez participantes, sete passaram a perceber melhor a luz e quatro conseguiram localizar objetos, achar portas e seguir linhas no chão. Ainda não dá para ler ou reconhecer rostos, mas é um passo concreto para quem não enxergava nada.

Painted wind turbines can dramatically reduce bird deaths, study finds

Mongabay

At a wind farm in Cádiz, Spain, researchers painted one of the three blades black on four turbines and compared them with 16 untouched neighbours, using more than a decade of daily carcass surveys. Bird deaths at the painted turbines fell by nearly 74%.

The trick is simple: spinning white blades blur into near invisibility, and one dark blade breaks up the blur so birds can see the danger. Studies in Norway and South Africa found similar drops, though one in the Netherlands didn't.

A can of paint helping clean energy and birds get along.

These women are bringing Darwin's rheas back to Chilean Patagonia

Mongabay

After a century of sheep ranching and endless fences, Darwin's rheas, the smaller southern cousin of our ema, had dwindled to perhaps 11 to 15 birds in Chile's Chacabuco Valley by 2016. Now a women-led team of rangers with Rewilding Chile breeds them and releases them in Patagonia National Park.

Thirteen more birds went free this year after two months of getting used to the wind and cold. Freed from their boxes, they started grazing right away and soon wandered kilometres off, which the team calls a very good sign.

One ranger calls the rhea "the gardener of the steppe", since it spreads seeds wherever it walks.

Aspens 'remember' past drought in their leaves, 3-year experiment finds

Phys.org

University of Utah biologists grew aspens from five forests in a shared garden and controlled their water for three years, putting some through drought. Long after the soil was wet again, the stressed trees kept making more of certain defensive chemicals in their leaves.

Those trees had less canopy damage, even the fungi living on their leaves changed, and the extra protection didn't seem to slow their growth. It's a kind of chemical memory, and it may help this struggling Western tree cope with a drier future.

NPR-Inspired Homework Leads Teen to Find Hidden Message a Decade After a Stranger's Kindness

Good News Network

When Rika Matsuda was seven, in Japan, the brakes on her new bike failed and she crashed into a wall. A stranger helped her up, cleaned her scrape, let her call her grandfather and, before leaving, taught her to fold a paper crane, which she kept on her desk for years.

Ten years later, a school assignment inspired by NPR's "My Unsung Hero" series made her finally unfold it. Inside, he had written two characters for a Japanese saying: treasure each meeting, because it may never come again.

A message that waited a decade for the right moment to be read.

Parceria entre USP e Yale possibilitou a recriação de uma música em tupi antigo

Jornal da USP

Em agosto de 1619, estudantes do Colégio de Santo Antão, em Lisboa, encenaram uma peça para o rei Filipe III, e no meio dela havia um coro cantado em tupi antigo: o Chorus Brasilicus. A letra sobreviveu num relato de 1620, que a pesquisadora Lisa Voigt, de Yale, encontrou na Biblioteca Nacional de Portugal em 2006. A melodia, não.

Com Íris Kantor, da USP, um pesquisador de tupi e dois musicólogos portugueses, a equipe criou três versões possíveis da música, partindo de uma melodia da época. Elas foram apresentadas no MAC USP, e comunidades indígenas que falam tupi já se interessaram pelo projeto.

Uma canção brasileira de mais de 400 anos voltando a ser cantada.

Could This Dutch Invention Help Tackle Plastic Pollution in America's Waterways?

Smithsonian Magazine

The Bubble Barrier is a perforated tube laid diagonally along a riverbed that blows a curtain of air bubbles to the surface. Floating plastic gets nudged to one side and the current carries it into a trap, while fish swim through and boats sail right over.

The first one, in Amsterdam since 2019, catches about 86% of floating plastic, from tiny bits to menus and selfie sticks. Lansing, Michigan, is now studying whether to install the first one in the United States.

Wood Street Steps

Atlas Obscura

When Philadelphia was young, William Penn agreed in 1684 that the public should always be able to walk down to the Delaware River, so a series of public stairways was built along the bluff.

There were more than ten of them once. The Wood Street Steps are the last ones still standing, a quiet little leftover of a 340-year-old promise that the riverfront belonged to everyone.

Woo Jin Joo Conjures Mythic Tales from Uncanny Sculptures of Fiber and Found Objects

Colossal

Seoul-born, London-based artist Woo Jin Joo collects discarded furniture, clothes and fabric on her walks and turns them, with clay and embroidered thread, into human-sized creatures. Fringed figures slump on the floor, and a pair of legs ends in a wooden table.

Drawing on East Asian myths and folklore, she treats ordinary objects as carriers of hidden stories. Her solo show Bodily Inbetweenness is on in London until 13 November.

Lost Italian Statue by Trevi Fountain Artist Discovered in Salvage Yard Among the Garden Ornaments

Good News Network

Among hundreds of old garden ornaments at a salvage yard in Wiltshire, England, a builder with an eye for antiques spotted a small marble figure and bought it for about £300. It turned out to be a signed model by Pietro Bracci, one of the sculptors of Rome's Trevi Fountain, made around 1736.

The 70 cm piece was his sample for a bronze statue ordered by Rome's Senate. After nearly three centuries and a stint as garden decoration, it's now heading to auction.

Barbara Ward's vision of planetary justice

Aeon · Or Rosenboim

Barbara Ward (1914–1981) was a British economist and writer who helped make "Spaceship Earth" a household image: one small, shared vessel whose passengers depend on each other. She was among the first to argue that prosperity, a healthy planet and fairness between people can't be pulled apart.

Or Rosenboim traces how her thinking changed over decades, from a faith-based idea of human community to the belief that caring for the environment could itself bring people together. A thoughtful intellectual-history piece, about 15 minutes of reading.
