🔬 Ciência & Espaço

Birds are more afraid of women than men, and nobody knows why

ScienceDaily

In parks across Czechia, France, Germany, Poland and Spain, researchers paired men and women matched for height and clothing and had them walk slowly toward birds. Across 37 species and some 2,700 trials, from sparrows to blackbirds, the birds took off sooner when a woman approached. Men got about a metre closer on average.

The team has no explanation yet. Their guesses include smell, body shape and the way people walk, and they stress that the result is still preliminary.

A little everyday mystery you can test on your next walk in the park.

Malaria Parasite Caught in the Act of Breaking and Entering

Quanta Magazine

A Columbia University lab used cryo-electron microscopy to image the "moving junction", the molecular doorway the malaria parasite builds to get into a red blood cell. The parasite anchors proteins that bend the cell's membrane with electric forces, so it can slip inside without bursting the cell.

Knowing exactly how that door works gives drug designers a new target, which matters as the parasite becomes resistant to current treatments. Related parasites, like the one behind toxoplasmosis, use similar machinery.

Curiosity spots strange wind-carved cliffs on Mars

ScienceDaily · NASA

On the slopes of Mount Sharp, Curiosity photographed a field of yardangs, ridges sculpted by the wind that stretch about 16 km. One layer looks out of place: the colour is wrong and its strata tilt at an odd angle. Scientists suspect it could be ancient volcanic ash.

The rover should get close enough to touch the cliffs with its robotic arm in 2027.

Fourteen years on Mars, and Curiosity is still finding things that puzzle its own team.

🌱 Natureza & Animais

This spider catapults ants

Mongabay

A newly described Australian "ballista spider" builds a spring-loaded silk trap meant for just one prey: the aggressive green tree ant. It seems the ant sets off the catapult itself when it attacks the snare.

It's the first known spider whose web is built to catch a single species, which is a striking level of evolutionary specialisation.

Googly-eyed buoys and kites help keep seabirds out of fishing nets

Mongabay

Diving seabirds such as guillemots and razorbills often drown in gillnets. Off the English coast, researchers tested two cheap scares: buoys painted with big staring eyes and kites shaped like birds of prey.

The eye buoys cut auk bycatch by 71% and the kites by 59%. Simple, low-tech tools like these are easy for fishers to adopt.

Sometimes conservation looks like a pair of giant cartoon eyes.

A New Wildlife Crossing in Colorado Has Already Cut Vehicle Collisions by 91 Percent

Colossal

Finished in December 2025, the grass-covered bridge over Interstate 25 is North America's largest crossing built only for animals. Elk, deer, bears and others now walk over the highway instead of running across it.

In less than a year, collisions on that stretch have dropped by 91%. That's safer for the animals and for drivers.

Mother bats' hearts beat faster at the sound of their own babies' cries

Phys.org

In a crowded colony, how does a mother bat find her own pup? Researchers studying Japanese house bats found that mothers recognise their pups' isolation calls, and their heart rate rises when they hear them.

That physical response helps make sure each mother nurses her own baby among hundreds of others.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Histórias Humanas

Estudantes de escola pública criam aplicativo que ajuda crianças a se comunicar

Só Notícia Boa

No campus de Jacareí do Instituto Federal de São Paulo, alunos criaram o Papuguinho, um app gratuito de comunicação aumentativa e alternativa. A criança toca em imagens, monta frases e o celular fala por ela. A ideia é ajudar crianças neurodivergentes, como as autistas, e outras com dificuldade de fala.

O projeto começou em 2024 com o estudante Gabriel Baroni e já é usado nas escolas municipais da cidade. Agora ficou em primeiro lugar no Prêmio Boas Práticas na Escola 2026, entre mais de 600 inscritos.

Adolescentes de escola pública dando voz, literalmente, a outras crianças.

Projeto da USP leva diálogos de Platão ao ensino médio por meio do teatro

Jornal da USP

Coordenado pelo professor Marcos Martinho, da FFLCH, o projeto leva universitários à ETEC Cepam, no campus Butantã, para fazer leituras dramáticas do Hípias Maior com alunos do 1º ano. No diálogo, Sócrates e Hípias tentam descobrir o que é o belo.

Durante dois meses, os universitários estudam a obra e depois ensaiam e debatem com as turmas. Aos poucos, a filosofia sai do livro e vira cena.

3 Italian "Hobbits" Searched Days for an Irishman's "Precious" Wedding Ring

Good News Network

Patrick Mohr, from Ireland, lost his gold wedding ring in a fast mountain stream in Italy while playing with his kid. Three young Italians, Filippo Palagi, Nicola Malloggi and Edoardo Poggi, spent three days searching with kayaks and snorkels. Finally Malloggi, roped to a boulder, saw a glint of gold in the sand.

They didn't just post it back. The three flew to Ireland to hand-deliver the ring, and the family welcomed them dressed as hobbits.

A real-life Tolkien quest that ends with the ring being given back.

Could the Voynich Manuscript's Pages Have Been Bound Out of Order This Whole Time?

Smithsonian Magazine

No one has been able to read the Voynich Manuscript, with its unknown script and strange plants. Lisa Fagin Davis and Colin Layfield compared text patterns statistically and studied clues like water stains and illustrations that don't line up across pages.

Their conclusion is that the work of five scribes is shuffled in a way no medieval workshop would have done. The sheets were probably meant to be read in order and were nested wrongly when the book was bound, which could explain why codebreakers keep getting stuck.

A Viking Age Coin So Rare It's Already a "National Treasure"

Smithsonian Magazine

In April, metal detectorists in eastern Norway found the Mørstad hoard, about 5,500 Viking Age silver coins. While cataloguing it, archaeologist Anne Skogsfjord found a coin minted under King Olaf II Haraldsson, carrying his portrait and Christian symbols.

Fewer than 20 of these coins are known anywhere in the world, and only one other has turned up in Norway since 1924. It's a small, physical trace of the king who brought Christianity to the country.

The Façade That Outlived Its Building, in Madrid

Atlas Obscura

On a Madrid street, between ordinary apartment blocks, stands the ornate front of an old marble shop, Mármoles Molina. The building behind it was demolished, but heritage rules protected the façade, so it stayed.

Now it stands there almost by stubbornness, a decorative shopfront left over from another Madrid.

🎨 Arte & Design

See Inside Cells Like Never Before in These Vivid Microscopic Images

Quanta Magazine

Quanta collects the best of the 2026 Nikon Small World competition, where science and art meet. Mouse brain neurons look like fireworks, lab-grown gut tissue looks like coral, and the cilia on a frog embryo's skin show up in neon.

The motion prize went to Patrick Hickey's time-lapse of mitochondria weaving between chloroplasts inside a plant cell.

Proof that some of the most beautiful landscapes are a few microns wide.

Revisit Claes Oldenburg's Seminal "Six Themes" 50 Years After Its Debut

Colossal

In 1975 a survey at the Walker Art Center brought together six of the Pop artist's recurring subjects: the Geometric Mouse, Three-Way Plug, Fagends, Clothespin, Typewriter Eraser, and Standing Mitt with Ball. From November 5 to December 19, Paula Cooper Gallery in New York shows them together again for the first time in five decades.

Some of the objects he enlarged, like the typewriter eraser, are now obsolete. As a result the sculptures have drifted, by accident, toward abstraction.

23 de outubro de 1906: o 14-bis sai do chão

Wikipédia

Há exatos 120 anos, no Campo de Bagatelle, em Paris, Alberto Santos-Dumont fez o 14-bis decolar com os próprios meios, diante de uma multidão. Ele voou cerca de 60 metros a poucos metros de altura e ganhou o prêmio Archdeacon. Foi um dos primeiros voos de um avião reconhecidos oficialmente. Não é à toa que 23 de outubro é o Dia do Aviador no Brasil.

📖 Leitura longa

Traction!

Aeon

Tribology, the study of friction, isn't a field most people think about. In this essay a tribologist goes from the invention of the wheel to the motion of planets to show how much depends on resistance.

The argument is that a world without friction would not be smooth progress. Nothing could grip, start or stop, and nothing would move forward.

A good weekend essay that changes how ordinary things look.
