O que são buracos brancos?

Superinteressante

Se um buraco negro é o lugar de onde nada sai, o buraco branco seria o contrário: um lugar onde nada consegue entrar e de onde matéria e energia só jorram para fora. As equações de Einstein permitem os dois, mas só os buracos negros foram observados de verdade — os brancos seguem como pura possibilidade matemática. A coluna explica ainda a ideia de que os dois poderiam estar ligados por um “buraco de minhoca”.

Um passeio rápido pelo lado mais estranho da relatividade — perfeito para o primeiro gole de café.

Mountain growth spurts may have sparked complex life on Earth

Phys.org

By reading metamorphic rocks spanning 3 billion years, a team led by Matthijs Smit (UBC) found that Earth's mountains grew taller in two distinct spurts — about 2.2 billion and 700 million years ago. Each spurt lines up with a major rise in atmospheric oxygen. The idea: taller mountains eroded faster, washing nutrients like phosphorus into the sea, feeding photosynthesis and, eventually, more complex life.

Mountains as the planet's fertilizer factories — a lovely way to think about the scenery.

New dinosaur species that lived more than 100 million years ago identified in the United Kingdom

Jornal da USP

Fossils dug up in 2001 at a clay quarry in southern England, and stored ever since at London's Natural History Museum, turned out to be a new plant-eating iguanodontian: Paludohadros proudlovii. The study was led by paleontologist Ricardo Sacamoto, from USP Ribeirão Preto. The juvenile was about 4 meters long and weighed roughly 1.5 tonnes, and it is the first iguanodontian described from that site.

A Brazilian scientist finding a new dinosaur hiding in a museum drawer, 25 years after the dig.

Fruit bats emerge as unlikely allies in the Atlantic Forest restoration

Mongabay

For two decades, researchers in Paraná have studied how fruit-eating bats can replant the Atlantic Forest. Unlike most birds, bats drop seeds mid-flight, scattering anywhere from 600 to over 25,000 seeds per hectare per night — even over degraded land. The team now uses fruit scents and essential oils to lure bats into areas that need restoring, and most seeds they bring are from fast-growing pioneer plants.

Night-shift gardeners with wings, working for free in our own backyard.

Spicy ping-pong balls and flaming dung keep elephants at bay in Zimbabwe

Mongabay

A Zimbabwean village protects its crops with a step-by-step, non-lethal defense against elephants: first fences hung with chili-soaked rags, then smoldering dung laced with chili oil. Only if that fails comes the last resort — a homemade launcher that fires chili-filled ping-pong balls. The last elephant to get one in the face stayed away for more than a year.

Creative, cheap and kind to the elephants — conflict resolution at its spiciest.

Por que alguns cachorros não gostam de olhar nos olhos? É um sinal de respeito ou de submissão?

Superinteressante

Desviar o olhar não é bem “respeito” nem “submissão”: é o cachorro escolhendo deixar a situação menos desconfortável — ou simplesmente dizendo que não está a fim de interagir agora. Estudos mostram que os cães leem muito bem nossa direção do olhar, a posição da cabeça e as expressões. O contato visual, no fim, funciona como um convite para conversar.

Vai olhar diferente para o seu (ou para o do vizinho) depois dessa.

The Areni-1 Cave Shoe

Atlas Obscura

In 2008, archaeologists in an Armenian cave found a leather shoe radiocarbon-dated to around 3600–3500 BC — one of the oldest known shoes in the world. It survived in near-perfect shape thanks to the cave's cool, dry air and protective layers of sheep dung. Today it can be seen in Yerevan.

Someone laced this up more than 5,500 years ago — and it still looks like a shoe.

This Four-Person NASA Crew Spent Over a Year on ‘Mars.’ Here's What They Did for 378 Days

Smithsonian Magazine

Four volunteers just stepped out of a 3D-printed, Mars-like habitat in Houston after 378 days inside. They rehearsed Martian surface walks, lived with the communication delays a real Mars crew would face, and grew some of their own food. It's a ground-based dress rehearsal for how people might one day live and work on the red planet.

A year of practice for one of humanity's biggest trips.

A Previously Unknown Landscape by Renaissance Master Albrecht Altdorfer Emerges After 70 Years

Smithsonian Magazine

Albrecht Altdorfer helped invent landscape painting as a subject in its own right, and now a work scholars didn't know existed has surfaced after 70 years in a private collection. Painted around 1520, it shows a dense forest with castle ruins beneath a rainbow. It is one of only three pure-landscape oil paintings known by the artist.

Five centuries old and brand new to art history at the same time.

Peter Morris Architects' Pastel Pink ‘Cloud House’ Billows in Northwest London

Colossal

In Gospel Oak, London, two neighbors agreed to share a single whimsical façade: pink walls, robin's-egg-blue windows and doors, and puffy arches in relief that look like clouds. Proof that neighbors can agree on something — and that it can be delightful.

How would the Amazon react if it dried up? A 25-year-old field study has the answer

Mongabay · ~1.700 palavras

Since 2001, about 6,000 transparent panels have quietly diverted half the rain falling on one hectare of the Caxiuanã National Forest, in Pará — one of the longest-running ecology experiments in the tropics, led by Brazilian and British scientists. The big trees started dying after about five years, and the plot lost roughly 40% of its biomass. But then something surprising happened: the smaller trees took over the leftover water, stopped the die-off and started growing again, settling into a new, thinner balance rather than turning into savanna.

A patient, quarter-century experiment that shows both how fragile and how stubborn the forest is.
