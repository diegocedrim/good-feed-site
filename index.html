Chuva de meteoros do cometa Halley tem pico nesta semana

Só Notícia Boa

As Oriônidas, poeira deixada pelo cometa Halley, atingem o pico entre a noite de quarta (21) e a madrugada de quinta (22), visíveis em todo o Brasil a olho nu. Em boas condições dá para ver de 10 a 20 meteoros por hora, que entram na atmosfera a cerca de 66 km por segundo. A dica é procurar as Três Marias, em Órion, longe das luzes da cidade, e esperar a Lua se pôr antes do amanhecer.

O Halley só volta em 2061, mas os pedacinhos dele aparecem todo ano.

Astronomers uncover new secrets of the Universe's first five minutes

ScienceDaily

A team led by the University of Minnesota spent 130 hours at the Large Binocular Telescope looking at 15 small, distant dwarf galaxies that have barely changed since the Big Bang. By reading more than ten helium lines and fifteen hydrogen lines at once, they pinned down how much helium was forged in the first minutes of the cosmos to 0.5% precision, about three times better than before. That number is a direct test of the Standard Model and of how many neutrino types existed back then.

Pristine galaxies working as time capsules from 13.8 billion years ago.

Japan just launched a mission to fetch samples from a moon of Mars

Smithsonian Magazine

Nobody knows for sure how Mars got its two little moons, Phobos and Deimos: captured asteroids, or debris from a giant impact? Japan's space agency JAXA has launched its MMX spacecraft to find out. It should arrive at the Martian system in 2027 and bring a sample of Phobos back to Earth in 2031.

The first time anyone will hold a piece of a Martian moon.

Six new calves offer hope for Cambodia's Irrawaddy dolphins

Mongabay

Six calves were born this year to the critically endangered Irrawaddy dolphins living in a 120 km stretch of the Mekong in northeastern Cambodia, bringing the population to about 118. The count comes from the country's Fisheries Administration and WWF-Cambodia, who have worked together since 2014. Gillnets and dams remain real threats, which is why local river guards keep watch every day.

Six babies matter a lot when the whole family fits in a school auditorium.

Scientists discover a bizarre Jurassic "squid" unlike anything ever seen

ScienceDaily

Meet Wyoteuthis linsterorum, a 160-million-year-old belemnite from Wyoming whose fossil guard is 10 cm long and an astonishing 6 cm wide: the chunkiest one ever found. Only two specimens exist, discovered almost 30 years apart; the second, found in 2019, finally allowed scientists to describe it. It seems to be a late survivor of a belemnite lineage thought to have vanished long before.

A barrel-shaped squid cousin, proudly named "the fattest belemnite".

Mozambique protects the "Butterfly Forest" of Mount Mabu

Good News Network

Some 23,000 acres around Mount Mabu are now a Community Conservation Area, managed with the local people who already treat the mountains as sacred. The forest, nicknamed "the butterfly forest" by David Attenborough, hosts more than 200 butterfly species plus creatures found nowhere else, like the Mount Mabu forest viper. Researcher Julian Bayliss spent two decades documenting it.

Possibly the largest continuous block of its forest type in southern Africa, now safe.

This dog learned to sniff out "sea vomit" and won Animal of the Year

Smithsonian Magazine

Sgiath, a Labrador from Scotland, was trained to detect the carpet sea squirt, a slimy invasive species nicknamed "sea vomit" that smothers British marine habitats. Her nose helps find it early, before it spreads. The work earned her an animal award from the International Fund for Animal Welfare.

A very good girl doing marine biology.

Archaeologists find the "smoking gun" of a Norse church in Greenland

Smithsonian Magazine

Near the farm of Erik the Red, the Viking who founded the Norse settlements in Greenland, archaeologists had long suspected a church once stood. Burials found at the site have now confirmed it. The discovery could help explain one of the great puzzles of the North Atlantic: why these communities quietly disappeared in the 15th century.

A thousand-year-old mystery, one trowel at a time.

Peat bogs reveal ancient Europe's hidden metal networks

Phys.org

Peat bogs quietly trap airborne lead layer by layer, so a 2-meter core from France's Pilat Massif turned into a 6,000-year diary of mining. Matching its lead isotopes against thousands of ore samples, Hugo Delile's team traced early silver-lead mining to Greece around 3700 B.C. They also found that the Romans mined across France, Germany and Britain, not just in Spain as often assumed.

A swamp that kept the receipts of the ancient economy.

Ultrassom com joelho em movimento pode substituir ressonância para diagnosticar lesões na cartilagem

Jornal da USP

Pesquisadores da Faculdade de Medicina da USP pediram que o paciente dobrasse o joelho em vários ângulos durante o ultrassom, contornando a patela, que costuma esconder a cartilagem. Em 82 pacientes, o método acertou mais de 90% das vezes, quase igual à ressonância magnética. E custa entre 5% e 10% do valor dela, com aparelhos que já existem em postos de saúde.

Uma ideia simples que pode encurtar muita fila.

In "Aeolus," DRIFT suspends illuminated glass rods in a sculpture of the wind

Colossal

Dutch studio DRIFT (Lonneke Gordijn and Ralph Nauta) hung glass rods with tiny lights inside that respond to the movement of visitors, like a breeze you can see. Named after the Greek god of the winds, the piece only fully "exists" when someone walks through it. It's on view at Carpenters, in London's Ladbroke Hall, through October 25.

Glass and light chosen because, like wind, they're here and then gone.

Rithika Merchant summons ancient mythology and mysticism in "The Dreaming Realm"

Colossal

Mumbai painter Rithika Merchant fills symmetrical, dreamlike scenes with human-animal hybrids, luna moths and ravens, often set against floods and eruptions. The show at Galerie LJ in Paris (through November 21) is about interdependence, cooperation between species and sharing knowledge. It manages to be both mystical and quietly hopeful.

Folk-tale imagery reimagined for our moment.

How intuitions work

Aeon · Marc A. Moffett · ~25 min

Philosopher Marc Moffett takes everyday gut feelings seriously: the sense that a stranger is off, or that an email is fishy. He argues that intuitions aren't irrational hunches but work much like perception, presenting the world to us as being a certain way, and can rightly justify what we believe. A patient, clear essay about how much of our reasoning runs on this quiet background sense.

Good company for a slow second cup.
