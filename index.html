Chuva de meteoros Leônidas ilumina o céu nesta madrugada

Só Notícia Boa

Todo novembro a Terra cruza a trilha de poeira deixada pelo cometa 55P/Tempel-Tuttle, e o resultado são as Leônidas. O melhor horário para ver no Brasil é entre 2h e o amanhecer desta quarta (18), olhando para o leste e o nordeste, onde fica a constelação de Leão. Em condições ideais, até uns 15 meteoros por hora.

Dica: lugar escuro, nada de celular e 20–30 minutos para os olhos se acostumarem. Telescópio só atrapalha.

The Ghostly Rocks of 'Idubi' — Bright Remnants of Aqueous Systems from Long Ago

NASA Science · Perseverance blog

Exploring ancient crust outside Jezero crater, Perseverance spotted a cluster of oddly pale rocks. SuperCam's laser data show they aren't volcanic at all but rich in hydrated silica — a mineral that on Earth forms in hot springs, soils and groundwater. Earlier silica finds were too small to study up close; these are big enough for a full close-up campaign, already underway.

Why it's cool: on Earth, silica deposits are excellent at preserving microfossils.

Uranus and Neptune's bizarre magnetic fields may finally be explained

ScienceDaily

Since Voyager 2 flew by in the 1980s, the ice giants' magnetic fields have looked wrong: Uranus's is tilted about 59° and Neptune's about 47°, versus roughly 11° for Earth. A UT Austin–led team suggests that crushing pressure deep inside breaks water into ions, so the interior grows more electrically conductive with depth — and their dynamo simulations reproduce the strange shapes.

Why it's cool: the models hint the poles might wander tens of degrees within decades — something the James Webb telescope may be able to check.

🌱 Natureza & Animais

Humpback whale snot unlocks whole-genome sequencing without invasive sampling

Phys.org

Off British Columbia, researchers flew drones carrying sterile petri dishes through the exhaled "blow" of humpback whales — no darts, no biopsies, often launched straight from shore. From 58 samples of 26 whales they sequenced whole genomes that matched traditional biopsies, correctly sexed every animal and even found new mitochondrial variants.

Why it's cool: the leftover microbes in each sneeze offer a peek at whale lung health — and the team now wants to know if whales that feed together are family or just old friends.

Hidden ecosystem: Scientists discover 245 animals living inside a single deep-sea rock

ScienceDaily

Metal-rich nodules sit on the Pacific seafloor 4,000–6,000 metres down, growing just a few millimetres per million years. Peering into the cracks of 244 of them, scientists counted more than 4,100 tiny animals — mostly roundworms and copepods — with one record nodule hosting 245. These miniature communities differ from the surrounding mud, yet look alike across sites up to 1,000 km apart.

Why it's cool: a rock the size of a potato turns out to be a whole apartment building.

These Adorable Hatchlings Are From the U.S. West Coast's First Known Natural Sea Turtle Nests

Smithsonian Magazine

Sea turtles had never been known to nest naturally on the U.S. West Coast — until two nests turned up this year. With a tropical storm approaching, the eggs were carefully moved to an aquarium to incubate, and the hatchlings have now been released on the very beaches where their mothers laid them.

Why it's cool: a small "first" that scientists will be watching closely in the coming years.

Empresa paga até 15º salário para funcionários que leem 12 livros por ano

Só Notícia Boa

No Mato Grosso, o Grupo Cometa, rede de concessionárias de motos, inclui a leitura nos critérios da participação nos lucros: quem lê 12 livros de pelo menos 200 páginas no ano e entrega um resumo pode ganhar um 14º ou 15º salário. As lojas têm bibliotecas com mais de 11 mil títulos, e a ideia existe formalmente desde 2006.

Por que é bom: o atual CEO começou na empresa ainda adolescente, no apoio administrativo — a história combina com o programa.

Farmer Finds Unknown Seeds in His Attic – and Grows an Ancient 'Miracle' Grain

Good News Network

Claudio Grossi, who farms near Parma on land his great-grandfather bought in 1915, found shrivelled wheat seeds in the attic and tossed them on a manure pile, half as a joke. They sprouted tall, with seven ears per stalk instead of one. University of Parma scientists identified an old Mediterranean wheat that had faded from Italian fields decades ago.

Why it's cool: it shrugs off weeds and drought with little fertilizer — there's now a flour called "Miracolo."

The People Who Became Wildlife

Reasons to be Cheerful

In Somerset, eighteen volunteers spent months trying to see their river through other eyes — a kestrel's, a salmon's, an otter's, even an earthworm's — sitting still and focusing on a single sense. One retiree realised the local meadow was too disturbed for kestrels; others counted more than 337 weirs blocking salmon along the River Tone. Their testimonies fed into a risk assessment and helped inspire Somerset's first River Rights Charter.

Why it's cool: empathy as a planning tool, literally.

🛠️ Tecnologia, História & Curiosidades

25 anos da Wikipédia: o que podemos aprender com o site (muito além dos verbetes)

Superinteressante

No século 15, a Enciclopédia Yongle exigiu 2 mil especialistas e virou 22 mil volumes manuscritos que só o imperador e poucos sábios podiam ler. Ela foi a maior do mundo por 600 anos — até ser superada, por volta de 2007, por um site escrito por voluntários. A Super mostra como a Wikipédia, hoje em quase 350 línguas, se organiza: a maioria faz pequenos ajustes, uma minoria escreve muito, e um conjunto de regras evita o caos.

Por que é bom: um raro exemplo de internet que deu certo graças a gente comum.

Ancient Humans May Have Hunted Hippos With Poisoned Spear Tips on the Arabian Peninsula 125,000 Years Ago

Smithsonian Magazine

Hippos in what is now the Saudi desert? 125,000 years ago, southern Arabia held lakes, and a new study suggests people hunted and butchered game beside one of them — possibly with poison-tipped spears. That would mean early humans didn't just hurry through the peninsula on their way out of Africa, but settled in and made a living there.

Why it's cool: a "green Arabia" rewrites the map of our ancestors' journey.

Practice Mindfulness with Roeselien Raimond's Portraits of Relaxed Winter Foxes

Colossal

Photographer Roeselien Raimond's series "Zen & The Art of Winter Foxes" shows foxes curled up and dozing in quiet snow. It began with a chance meeting with a vixen during a snowstorm and plays with the Zen idea that chasing something too hard pushes it away.

Why it's cool: the calmest thing you'll see today. Possibly this week.

Why Are All 37 of the Picture Frames in This Exhibition Empty? The Frames Themselves Are the Stars of the Show

Smithsonian Magazine

At the Art Gallery of Ontario, 37 frames hang on the walls with nothing inside them. The display flips the usual hierarchy of the museum, inviting visitors to notice the carving, gilding and craft that normally go unseen around the paintings they hold.

Why it's cool: a museum that asks you to look at the part you always skip.

17 de novembro de 1910: nasce Rachel de Queiroz

Wikipédia

Nascida em Fortaleza, Rachel de Queiroz publicou O Quinze em 1930, quando ainda nem tinha completado 20 anos — um romance sobre a grande seca de 1915 no Ceará que virou clássico da literatura brasileira. Em 1977, tornou-se a primeira mulher eleita para a Academia Brasileira de Letras, e em 1993 foi também a primeira mulher a receber o Prêmio Camões.

Boa ideia para o fim de semana: O Quinze é curto e se lê numa sentada.

📖 Leitura longa

What Makes Up the Cosmos? A 20-Year Survey Hints That Something Is Missing.

Quanta Magazine · Charlie Wood

The Dark Energy Survey spent two decades mapping galaxies with a 570-megapixel camera on a Chilean mountaintop, and its final results are in. Combined with other data, they lean — not yet decisively — toward a universe in which dark energy, two-thirds of everything, is slowly weakening. Charlie Wood walks through why the mismatch keeps showing up across datasets, and what it would mean if our best model of the cosmos needs rewriting.

Why it's worth it: a clear, patient tour of one of the biggest open questions in physics.
