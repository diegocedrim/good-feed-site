The narwhal's legendary "unicorn horn" holds a remarkable engineering secret

ScienceDaily

Using X-ray tomography at three European synchrotrons, researchers peered inside narwhal tusks and found two microscopic spirals twisting in opposite directions: the outer layer winds one way, the dentin inside winds the other. Like the counter-twisted strands of a rope, the arrangement makes the tusk, which can grow up to two metres, remarkably resistant to twisting forces. The work was published in Nature Communications.

Por que é legal: o "chifre de unicórnio" é, na verdade, uma corda de engenharia natural.

ScienceDaily

ESA's Euclid telescope was built to map dark matter, but a PhD student at the University of Helsinki, Mikko Pöntinen, ran its images through software he wrote and spotted a moving smudge: a brand-new comet, now officially C/2026 S1 (Euclid). Mongabay

In 1984, a beardfish caught off Japan's Ogasawara Islands turned out to have an 8 cm cardinalfish in its stomach. The specimen sat in a Tokyo museum for 42 years until researchers realised it was unknown to science. It is now Epigonus masamunei, named after the legendary swordsmith Masamune because its curved spine resembles one of his blades.

Making Peace With a Himalayan 'Ghost'

Reasons to be Cheerful

In 45 villages of Ladakh, herders used to kill snow leopards that raided their livestock. A local trust helped them build predator-proof corrals with stone walls and mesh roofs, and set up homestays and crafts so that the cats became a source of income. Retaliatory killings have stopped there, and part of the tourism money now goes into village conservation funds.

Phys.org

A team led by CNRS archaeologist Isabelle Hairy combined nearly 5,000 stone blocks mapped by divers with coins, mosaics and medieval Arabic descriptions to build the first scientifically complete 3D model. It reveals 67 rooms on nine levels and a ramp wide enough for two riders side by side.

Phys.org

British Museum researchers analysed seven crocodile mummies, sacred to the god Sobek. One of them, nearly 4 metres long, carries more than 25 hatchlings on its back, just as baby crocodiles ride on their mothers.

The energy of thought

Aeon · David Colaço & Philipp Haueis · ~3.000 palavras

The brain is about 2% of our body mass yet burns roughly 20% of our calories, running on around 20 watts. Philosophers of science Colaço and Haueis argue that almost every model of the mind ignores this — how the brain gets its energy, keeps its balance and clears its waste — and that taking metabolism seriously changes which theories of memory and mental effort can be true.
