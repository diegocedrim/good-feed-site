Astronomers discover a ghostly green star unlike any known before

ScienceDaily

Astronomers at the University of Warwick have found WD J1330+6435, the first white dwarf that genuinely looks green. Sodium in its dense atmosphere soaks up orange light, leaving it a pale chartreuse. Surveying 151 nearby white dwarfs, the team also spotted pink, purple, yellow and cyan ones, and estimates about one in thirty white dwarfs has an unusual colour.

Just 3,000 steps a day may help slow Alzheimer's, scientists find

ScienceDaily

Researchers followed almost 300 older adults and found that walking as few as 3,000 steps a day was linked to slower buildup of tau, one of the proteins tied to Alzheimer's. The more active participants showed close to half the cognitive decline, and the benefits seemed to level off at 5,000–7,500 steps. Amyloid plaques didn't change; the protection showed up in tau in memory-related brain regions.

Rolling without wheels: How dwarf reedsnakes coordinate their bodies for high-speed escapes

Phys.org

Small Southeast Asian snakes called dwarf reedsnakes escape danger by rolling. A Toho University team filmed them alternating left and right, rolling onto one side and swinging an arched loop of body past the head, all while keeping the head steady and on course. They hit up to 3.7 metres per second, about four times faster than their normal slither.

Why are these lynxes washing their food?

Mongabay

Over five years, camera traps in Spain caught several female Iberian lynxes dipping dead rabbits into water troughs before eating them, something nobody had documented in a wild cat. Maybe it's a way to get extra water in hot summers, or to soften meals for cubs. Or maybe it's something else. Scientists honestly don't know yet.

Por que é legal: uma espécie que quase sumiu agora está aí, inventando moda.

A simple touch can bring dying corals back to life in just 7 weeks

ScienceDaily

Stanford researchers put severely bleached corals in physical contact with healthy, genetically identical corals. The tissues fused and passed along the symbiotic algae corals need, and the bleached ones fully recovered in seven weeks. Sometimes five days of contact was enough to start the healing. It won't work reef-wide yet, but it's the fastest bleaching recovery ever recorded.

Volunteers became salmon, deer and earthworms. Then they set out to save their river

Positive News

In Somerset, England, eighteen volunteers spent time imagining life along the River Tone as a salmon, a deer, an earthworm, an otter or a kestrel. Seeing the river through animal eyes made pollution and habitat damage hard to ignore. As one participant said, "once you see it, you can't unsee it." Come Face-to-Face with the Lion Nebula in the James Webb Space Telescope's Portrait

Colossal

Webb's infrared portrait of NGC 2392 shows a dying star shedding its outer layers into a glowing bubble of gas that looks like a lion's face. The bright core is the nose and the shell is the mane. You can see where the star's radiation is destroying dust and where delicate filaments are holding on. In about 10,000 years the whole thing will have drifted away.
